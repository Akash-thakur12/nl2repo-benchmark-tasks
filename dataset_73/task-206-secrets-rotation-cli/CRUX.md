# Why this task is hard, and why it is still fair

Reviewer-facing note. Every claim below is checkable by running
`python tools/check_shortcuts.py` and `python tools/check_crux.py`.

The task is unchanged in shape: replay a secret-rotation capture and report the
events, the refusals and the final version state. What changed is where the
answers live. Four sentences that used to hand over an answer have been deleted
from `environment/docs/rotation-spec.md` and re-shipped as evidence: the
specification now names `environment/archive/` as authoritative, and the archive
holds one real capture beside the live runner's own log for that run. The work
is the same size; less of it is transcription.

**Authority ordering, so nothing is ambiguous.** `rotation-spec.md` states:
"`archive/` holds one capture from the live runner beside that run's own log.
Where any other document in this repository disagrees with the log, the log is
what the runner did." `rot-4417.runner.log` is generated by
`tools/make_sessions.py` from `tests/reference_rotation.py`, so it is ground
truth by construction and cannot drift.

---

## Crux E - the replay clock is a high-water mark

**Wrong default.** `self.now = record["t"]` - the line every implementer writes,
and what the previous reference and solution both did.

**Why it is invisible.** Timestamps in all six samples and all 25 original
held-out captures are non-decreasing, so the raw assignment reproduces
`samples/expected.json` byte for byte.

**Evidence in environment/.** `docs/session-format.md` now names the hazard
without the rule: a capture is merged from the runner's journal and the fleet's
acknowledgement stream, so a record may carry a `t` earlier than the one before
it. `archive/rot-4417.session` contains exactly that - `ack t=180` arriving after
`tick t=250` - and `archive/rot-4417.runner.log` reads `250 complete dev v2` /
`250 stage staging v2`. Only the clamp survives that pairing.

**Tests that catch it.** `test_clock_never_runs_backwards` (h26, h28),
`test_clock_clamp_across_a_grace_deadline` (h27), `test_rollback_order` (h28),
plus the two whole-corpus comparisons. h27 diverges on `state.live`
(`[1, 2]` correct, `[2]` wrong), not only on timestamps.

## Crux I - a grace period serves the window it was granted

**Wrong default.** Keep `grace_seconds` on the engine and read the current value
inside the sweep, so a mid-session `policy` record retunes a countdown that is
already running. "It applies from now on" is the obvious reading.

**Why it is invisible.** No sample contains a `policy` record at all.

**Evidence in environment/.** `rotation-spec.md` 8.0 says only that a `policy`
record sets `grace_seconds` for the fleet from that moment; it says nothing about
countdowns already running. The archive settles it: v1 is granted 600s at
t=1000, `policy grace_seconds=120` lands at t=1100, `tick 1220` produces nothing
in the log, and the log reads `1600 revoked v1 (granted 1000, window 600)`. The
same log then revokes v3 at 1910 under a 120s window granted at 1790, so the
answer is "dated at grant", not "policy never applies".

**Tests that catch it.** `test_policy_does_not_retune_a_running_countdown`
(h29, h30) is the hard half; `test_policy_record_is_applied` (h31) is the easy
half - a policy that lands before any grant, which scores for anyone who parses
the record at all. h30 diverges on `state.live`.

## Crux H - stage membership is live, and entangled with rule 2.2

**Wrong default.** Snapshot `members_of(env)` into the rollout when the stage
begins. It looks like the safer engineering choice ("the cohort is fixed when
the wave starts").

**Why it is invisible.** Every consumer in the sample corpus is registered before
its `rotate` and never moves.

**Evidence in environment/.** `rotation-spec.md` 6.1 keeps its wording ("every
consumer registered in it") with no gloss about when membership is measured. The
archive shows both directions: `stg-1` moves out of `staging` at t=1720 and the
log completes `staging` on the move itself (`1720 complete staging v4`), while
`checkout-7` registers into `prod` at t=1740 and the log holds `complete prod v4`
back to 1790 - past `billing-api`'s ack at 1750 - waiting for the newcomer.
Neither line is explicable under a snapshot.

**Tests that catch it.** `test_late_registration_blocks_a_stage` (h32),
`test_membership_change_completes_a_stage` (h33, h34). h33 empties an
environment mid-stage; h34 moves a consumer out of the environment currently
staging, so the stage completes on who is left.

## Crux C - the environment argues for the wrong answers

`docs/postmortem-4417.md` is the on-call engineer's writeup of the archived
incident, and it is the most readable document in the environment. It asserts
three things the runner's own log contradicts: that the 120s policy at t=1100
took effect immediately and revoked early (crux I); that the runner back-dated
the `dev` completion to the acknowledgement's own `t=180` (crux E); and, in
passing, that the runner walks environments alphabetically (contradicting 2.1
and the log). It cites `tools/quicklook.py`, a runnable ~40-line scratch
summariser that stamps every line with the record's raw `t` and prints
`sorted(environments)` - so a model that runs the shipped tool gets the wrong
answers in a confident voice.

This is misdirection, never ambiguity, because the ordering is explicit: the
postmortem carries the header "Written from `tools/quicklook.py` output during
the incident ... Not reconciled against the runner log", quicklook's docstring
says it is not the runner and names the two assumptions it never checked, and
`rotation-spec.md` puts the log above every other document. A careful engineer
reconciles them; a skimmer implements the postmortem.

## Reward is continuous, and more so than before

`tests/test_outputs.py` went from 6 test functions to 21. The three original
whole-corpus comparisons are untouched (no coverage was removed); the 15 new
tests are per-mechanism groups, each with a docstring naming the rule it checks,
so a single misread edge costs that edge's tests instead of a third of the
grade. `tools/check_crux.py` runs the real suite against each wrong default and
asserts each one still passes most of it:

```
reference (unpatched)                                     samples pass   21/21
E: the clock is copied from the record, not clamped       samples pass   16/21
I: a policy change retunes a running countdown            samples pass   18/21
I: the policy record is ignored altogether                samples pass   18/21
H: stage membership is snapshotted when the stage begins  samples pass   17/21
```

Every one of those reproduces `samples/expected.json` exactly - which is why
success criterion 6 is a false green light - and every one still fails held-out
tests. `tools/check_shortcuts.py` carries the same discipline across 22
enumerated near-misses.

## Nothing new to implement

The reference delta is three ideas: one clamp
(`self.now = max(self.now, record["t"])`), one extra field on the grace tuple
(the window is dated at grant), and one non-change (do not snapshot membership).
The only new record type, `policy`, is three lines of parsing. `environment/`
still ships no implementation, so `/app/run.sh` is absent and the starting state
fails all 21 tests.
